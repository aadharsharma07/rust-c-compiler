# Lesson 2.5 — Arithmetic in Assembly
**Unit:** 2 — Expressions and an IR · **Type:** Builds arithmetic codegen (optional: bitwise ops) · **Est. time:** 3–5 h · **Tests:** ch. 3
**Prerequisites:** lessons 2.3, 2.4

## Learning goals
- Translate binary expressions to TACKY, evaluating the left operand before the right.
- Use two-operand x86-64 arithmetic (`addl`, `subl`, `imull`) and understand that the destination is also an input.
- Explain why division needs `cdq` and `idivl`, and where quotient and remainder end up.
- Extend the fix-up pass with the operand constraints of `idiv`, `imul`, `add` and `sub`.
- Keep large `match` expressions readable as the instruction set grows.

## Key ideas
**TACKY.** Add `Binary(binary_operator, val src1, val src2, val dst)` with operators
`Add | Subtract | Multiply | Divide | Remainder`. Translate `e1 op e2` by translating `e1`, then
`e2`, then emitting one `Binary` into a fresh temporary. (C leaves operand evaluation order
unspecified; left-to-right is a simple, legal choice.)

**Two-operand arithmetic.** x86-64 arithmetic overwrites its second (AT&T) operand:
`addl %ecx, %eax` means `eax = eax + ecx`; `subl $3, -4(%rbp)` means `mem = mem - 3`. Note the
order for `sub`: the *source* is subtracted *from* the destination. So
`dst = src1 op src2` lowers to `Mov(src1, dst)`, then `Binary(op, src2, dst)`.

**Division.** `idivl x` divides the 64-bit value `%edx:%eax` (high half in `%edx`) by `x`,
leaving the quotient in `%eax` and the remainder in `%edx`. `cdq` sign-extends `%eax` into
`%edx` first; without it, whatever garbage is in `%edx` becomes part of the dividend. So:

| TACKY | Assembly AST |
|---|---|
| `Binary(Divide, a, b, dst)` | `Mov(a, Reg AX)`, `Cdq`, `Idiv(b)`, `Mov(Reg AX, dst)` |
| `Binary(Remainder, a, b, dst)` | same, but finish with `Mov(Reg DX, dst)` |
| `Binary(Add/Subtract/Multiply, a, b, dst)` | `Mov(a, dst)`, `Binary(op, b, dst)` |

New assembly AST pieces: `Binary(binary_operator, operand, operand)`, `Idiv(operand)`, `Cdq`;
operators `Add | Sub | Mult`; registers gain `DX` and `R11`.

**Constraints and fix-ups** (scratch: `%r10d` for sources, `%r11d` for destinations):

| Illegal form | Fix |
|---|---|
| `idivl $3` (immediate operand) | `movl $3, %r10d` · `idivl %r10d` |
| `addl`/`subl` with both operands in memory | move the source to `%r10d` first |
| `imull x, -8(%rbp)` (memory destination) | `movl -8(%rbp), %r11d` · `imull x, %r11d` · `movl %r11d, -8(%rbp)` |

**Worked example.** `return 10 / 3 - 1;` → TACKY: `tmp.0 = 10 / 3`, `tmp.1 = tmp.0 - 1`, `Return(tmp.1)`.
Fixed-up body (between prologue and epilogue, `subq $8, %rsp`):
```
	movl	$10, %eax
	cdq
	movl	$3, %r10d
	idivl	%r10d
	movl	%eax, -4(%rbp)
	movl	-4(%rbp), %r10d
	movl	%r10d, -8(%rbp)
	subl	$1, -8(%rbp)
	movl	-8(%rbp), %eax
```
Exit status: `2`.

**Optional bitwise extension** (`--bitwise` tests): `&`, `|`, `^` map to `andl`, `orl`, `xorl`
(same constraints as `add`). Shifts `<<`/`>>` map to `sall`/`sarl` (arithmetic right shift for
signed `int`), whose count must be an immediate or `%cl` — so you'd add `CX` and a fix-up.
Precedences: `<<` `>>` 40, `&` 25, `^` 20, `|` 15.

## Questions the tutor should ask you
- Why does `Mov(src1, dst); Binary(Sub, src2, dst)` compute `src1 - src2` and not `src2 - src1`?
- What is in `%edx` before `cdq`, and what goes wrong for `-7 / 2` if you skip it?
- Why does `idiv` not need a destination operand?
- Which scratch register does each fix-up use, and why must they not clash within one rewritten sequence?
- What does C say about `2147483647 + 1`, and why do the tests avoid it?

## What you build
- **TACKY:** `Binary` instruction and binary-operator enum; translation of AST `Binary` nodes.
- **Assembly AST:** `Binary`, `Idiv`, `Cdq`; new registers `DX`, `R11`.
- **TACKY → assembly:** the lowering table above.
- **Pseudo replacement:** must now visit operands inside `Binary` and `Idiv` too.
- **Fix-up:** the three rules above (plus the existing `mov` rule).
- **Emitter:** `addl`, `subl`, `imull`, `idivl`, `cdq`; `%edx`, `%r11d`.
- **Unit tests:** TACKY for `1 + 2 * 3`; assembly after fix-up for `6 / 3`, `7 % 3`, `2 * 3` with
  memory destination; run small programs end to end and check exit codes.

## Common pitfalls
- Emitting `subl dst, src` (operands swapped): `5 - 3` becomes `-2`.
- Forgetting `cdq`, so division works for small positive numbers and fails mysteriously for negatives.
- Reading the result from `%eax` for `%` (it's in `%edx`).
- Forgetting to update pseudo replacement for the new instructions, leaving `Pseudo` operands that the emitter can't print.
- Applying the `imul` fix to `add`/`sub` (or vice versa) — each instruction has its own rule.
